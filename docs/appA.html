Appendix A

Notation and glossary

By relieving the brain of all unnecessary work, a good notation sets it free to concentrate on more advanced problems …

Alfred North Whitehead, An Introduction to Mathematics (1911)

This appendix is the reference for the whole book. Each symbol means one thing, each term has one name, and every chapter, figure, notebook and skill follows the tables below. Read Section A.1 once. Use Section A.2 to look up a symbol and Section A.3 to look up a word. The last column of each table points to the place where the entry is introduced.

A subscript makes a new symbol. EEE, EξE_\xiEξ​ and EℓE_\ellEℓ​ are three different things, and so are fff and fΔf_\DeltafΔ​, or PPP, PfP_fPf​ and PUUP_{UU}PUU​. The subscripts min⁡\minmin and max⁡\maxmax always mark a bound or an extreme value: τmax⁡\tau_{\max}τmax​, θmax⁡\theta_{\max}θmax​, Δmax⁡\Delta_{\max}Δmax​, Ψmax⁡\Psi_{\max}Ψmax​, ωmax⁡\omega_{\max}ωmax​, Jmin⁡J_{\min}Jmin​. A hat marks an estimate (R^\hat RR^, Δ^\hat\DeltaΔ^) and a tilde a prediction or estimation error (e~R\tilde e_Re~R​, Δ~\tilde\DeltaΔ~).

A few letters are local and are not listed in the tables: x,y,zx, y, zx,y,z for arbitrary vectors in an identity, AAA for an arbitrary matrix, i,j,ki, j, ki,j,k for indices, nnn and NNN for counts. So are Π\PiΠ for a projection, β\betaβ and σ\sigmaσ in two exercises of Chapter 3, Φ\PhiΦ for a transition matrix in one exercise of Chapter 7, and cic_ici​ for the entries of a matrix. They are named where they are used and mean nothing beyond that sentence. Section A.2.11 lists the letters that keep a second, local meaning inside one section because the alphabet ran out.

A.1 Conventions

Frames. The world frame does not move, with e3e_3e3​ pointing up. The body frame is attached to a rigid body. A rotation matrix RRR maps body-frame components to world-frame components.

Gravity. Gravity acts along −e3-e_3−e3​. A mass mmm at position xxx has potential energy mg e3⋅xm g\,e_3\cdot xmge3​⋅x and weight −mg e3-m g\,e_3−mge3​.

Body and world velocities. The angular velocity of a rigid body is Ω\OmegaΩ, expressed in the body frame, with R˙=R Ω∧\dot R = R\,\Omega^\wedgeR˙=RΩ∧. Its world-frame components are ω=R Ω\omega = R\,\Omegaω=RΩ. The angular velocity of a direction q∈S2q \in S^2q∈S2 is ω\omegaω, in the world frame, with q˙=ω×q\dot q = \omega\times qq˙​=ω×q and ω⋅q=0\omega\cdot q = 0ω⋅q=0. Positions xxx and velocities v=x˙v = \dot xv=x˙ are in the world frame.

Hat and vee. The hat map is written after its argument: x∧y=x×yx^\wedge y = x\times yx∧y=x×y and (x∧)∨=x(x^\wedge)^\vee = x(x∧)∨=x. On a twist, ζ∧\zeta^\wedgeζ∧ is the 4×44\times44×4 matrix of (2.10).

Twists. A twist is ordered linear part first, ζ=(V,Ω)\zeta = (V, \Omega)ζ=(V,Ω), both parts in the body frame.

Cable direction. For a cable-suspended load, qqq points from the quadrotor to the load, so xQ=xL−l qx_Q = x_L - l\,qxQ​=xL​−lq. The cable tension TTT is positive when the cable pulls.

Desired quantities. The subscript ddd marks the configuration or motion that a controller aims for: RdR_dRd​, qdq_dqd​, xdx_dxd​, Ωd\Omega_dΩd​, ωd\omega_dωd​, and also the force FdF_dFd​, the yaw ψd\psi_dψd​ and the input udu_dud​ or τd\tau_dτd​ that go with it. A fixed one is called the target. A moving one is called the reference. No other mark (“ref”, “des”, a star) is used.

Order of arguments. The actual configuration comes first and the desired one second, in formulas and in code: ΨR\Psi_RΨR​ is built from Rd⊤RR_d\T RRd⊤​R, and a function is called as e_R(R, Rd). An error is the actual quantity minus the desired one: ex=x−xde_x = x - x_dex​=x−xd​, eF=fR e3−Fde_F = f R\,e_3 - F_deF​=fRe3​−Fd​. For a prediction error the state predictor is the actual one and the vehicle the desired one, as in (8.5).

The tracking law. The attitude law is always written in the form of (6.2): the feedback part −kR eR−kΩ eΩ-k_R\,e_R - k_\Omega\,e_\Omega−kR​eR​−kΩ​eΩ​, then the feedforward Ω×JΩ−J(Ω×R⊤Rd Ωd−R⊤Rd Ω˙d)\Omega\times J\Omega - J(\Omega\times R\T R_d\,\Omega_d - R\T R_d\,\dot\Omega_d)Ω×JΩ−J(Ω×R⊤Rd​Ωd​−R⊤Rd​Ω˙d​), with the cross product written with ×\times×. On the sphere the gains kqk_qkq​, kωk_\omegakω​ multiply the errors in the angular acceleration, as in (6.12). Every chapter that restates these laws uses the same form and cites them.

Lyapunov functions. V0V_0V0​ is the energy-like function without a cross term and VVV the one with it. W1W_1W1​ and W2W_2W2​ bound VVV from below and above, W3W_3W3​ bounds its rate, all three as quadratic forms in z=(∥eR∥,∥eΩ∥)z = (\|e_R\|, \|e_\Omega\|)z=(∥eR​∥,∥eΩ​∥), and γ\gammaγ is the exponential rate that follows. Their entries depend on the cross term chosen and are stated with each result. The smallest and largest eigenvalues of a symmetric matrix are λmin⁡(⋅)\lambda_{\min}(\cdot)λmin​(⋅) and λmax⁡(⋅)\lambda_{\max}(\cdot)λmax​(⋅).

Gains. A gain is a kkk with the subscript of the error or estimate it multiplies: kRk_RkR​, kΩk_\OmegakΩ​, kqk_qkq​, kωk_\omegakω​, kxk_xkx​, kvk_vkv​, kΔk_\DeltakΔ​. The cutoff kfk_fkf​, the rate kVk_VkV​ and the slack weight kwk_wkw​ follow the same pattern. A gain on a prediction error carries a tilde. Capital KKK is a gain matrix.

Constraints and safety. A safe set is written {H≥0}\{H \ge 0\}{H≥0} with a barrier function HHH. A cone on the sphere has axis qcq_cqc​ and half-angle θmax⁡\theta_{\max}θmax​: a direction that must stay inside satisfies q⋅qc≥cos⁡θmax⁡q\cdot q_c \ge \cos\theta_{\max}q⋅qc​≥cosθmax​, and one that must stay outside satisfies q⋅qc≤cos⁡θmax⁡q\cdot q_c \le \cos\theta_{\max}q⋅qc​≤cosθmax​. A bound on an input is written with max⁡\maxmax, as in ∣τi∣≤τmax⁡|\tau_i| \le \tau_{\max}∣τi​∣≤τmax​. A slack variable is www.

Sampling and horizons. The time step hhh is also the sampling period of a controller. Stages along a horizon are indexed by k=0,…,Nk = 0, \dots, Nk=0,…,N, and a sampled quantity carries that index: sks_ksk​, AkA_kAk​, τd,k\tau_{d,k}τd,k​.

Optimization. A cost is Φ\PhiΦ. A step of an optimizer on a manifold is a variation used at finite size (η\etaη, ξ\xiξ, δΩ\delta\OmegaδΩ) and is applied by a retraction. grad⁡\operatorname{grad}grad and Hess⁡\operatorname{Hess}Hess are the first- and second-order coefficients along such a step (Definition 13.1). For a function on Rn\R^nRn they are the usual gradient and Hessian, and no other symbol is used for them.

Variations. A variation is written with δ\deltaδ. On SO(3)\SO(3)SO(3) it is δR=R η∧\delta R = R\,\eta^\wedgeδR=Rη∧ with η\etaη in the body frame and unconstrained. On S2S^2S2 it is δq=ξ×q\delta q = \xi\times qδq=ξ×q with ξ⋅q=0\xi\cdot q = 0ξ⋅q=0. The small parameter of a family of curves is ϵ\epsilonϵ.

Velocity variation on the sphere. With ω⋅q=0\omega\cdot q = 0ω⋅q=0 and ξ⋅q=0\xi\cdot q = 0ξ⋅q=0 kept along the whole curve, δω=ξ˙−2 ω×ξ\delta\omega = \dot\xi - 2\,\omega\times\xiδω=ξ˙​−2ω×ξ. This vector is not tangent: its tangent part is ξ˙−ω×ξ\dot\xi - \omega\times\xiξ˙​−ω×ξ and its part along qqq is −ω×ξ-\omega\times\xi−ω×ξ. See the pitfall.

Equations on the sphere. An equation of motion for q∈S2q \in S^2q∈S2 holds only after projection by I−qq⊤I - q q\TI−qq⊤, together with q⋅ω˙=0q\cdot\dot\omega = 0q⋅ω˙=0.

Signs of derived equations. Chapters write equations of motion with the inertia on the left, as in JΩ˙+Ω×JΩ=τJ\dot\Omega + \Omega\times J\Omega = \tauJΩ˙+Ω×JΩ=τ. The symbolic library of Chapter 5 returns the coefficient of each variation, Eξ=0E_\xi = 0Eξ​=0, with the inertia term negative, and its standard form is M v˙+b+G u=0M\,\dot v + b + G\,u = 0Mv˙+b+Gu=0.

Transposes and products. The transpose is R⊤R\TR⊤. The dot product of two vectors is a⋅ba\cdot ba⋅b, never a⊤ba\T ba⊤b. A transpose appears only in matrix expressions such as qq⊤q q\Tqq⊤ and s⊤Q ss\T Q\,ss⊤Qs. A norm is ∥x∥\|x\|∥x∥. The identity is III, with a subscript only when its size is not three.

Angles and units. Angles in formulas are in radians. Degrees are used in prose and on plot axes, written 90° in text and 90∘90^\circ90∘ in math. Units are SI and upright: rad/s, m/s², kg·m², N·m, N·m·s, N·s, s⁻¹. A percentage is written 5%. The natural logarithm of a number is ln⁡\lnln. log⁡\loglog is the matrix logarithm.

Spelling. American spelling: linearization, modeling, stabilize, center, behavior, maneuver, neighbor.

Names in code. Notebooks, skills and scripts use hat, vee, expm_so3, log_so3, Psi_R, Psi_q, e_R, e_Omega, e_q, e_omega, and write desired quantities as Rd, qd, Omegad, omegad. A symbol with a Greek subscript is spelled out: k_R, k_Omega, k_Delta, k_f, tau_fb, P_UU, nu_r. Outside the book the same notation is written in plain LaTeX, for example R_d^\top R.

Colors in figures. Ink is structure. Indigo is the main object or the method that works. Terracotta is the contrasting object, the error or the method that fails. Teal is a third role. The trace error is always indigo and the logarithm error always teal.

References, targets and constraints in figures. A target is an ink dot labeled “target”. A reference drawn alone is the main object and is indigo. A reference drawn next to a motion that tracks it steps back: a thin dashed gray line with a hollow marker, terracotta on the stretch where it violates a constraint. The motion that tracks it is a solid line with a filled marker, indigo for the controller that works and terracotta for the one that fails, and a plan or prediction is teal. The boundary of a constraint is ink, and the forbidden side is filled in a pale terracotta where it can be filled. The axis of a cone is ink and labeled qcq_cqc​. A quadrotor is two ink arms along R e1R\,e_1Re1​ and R e2R\,e_2Re2​ with four rotor rings.

A.3 Glossary

Action. The integral of the Lagrangian along a path. Section 4.1.

Adaptation gain. The gain kΔk_\DeltakΔ​ of the adaptation law. It sets how closely the vehicle follows the state predictor. (8.8).

Adjoint. Conjugation by a group element, acting on the Lie algebra. It changes the frame of a velocity. Section 2.2.1.

Almost-global stability. Convergence from every initial condition except a set of measure zero. It is the best a continuous controller can do on a compact configuration space. It promises no rate: an exponential rate holds only on a sublevel set. Section 1.4, Theorem 6.2.

Attitude. The rotation of a rigid body relative to the world frame, a point of SO(3)\SO(3)SO(3). Section 1.1.

Augmented barrier function. A barrier function for a position constraint that also depends on the attitude, so that the torque appears in its second derivative. (8.19).

Barrier function. A function HHH of the state whose nonnegative set is the safe set, and for which some input always achieves H˙≥−αH\dot H \ge -\alpha HH˙≥−αH. The control literature calls it a control barrier function. Section 8.6.

Barrier parameter. The weight μ\muμ of the logarithmic barrier in an interior-point method. Section 13.7.

Body angular velocity. The angular velocity Ω\OmegaΩ of a rigid body, expressed in the body frame. It is what a gyroscope reads. Section 2.2.1.

Body frame. The frame attached to a rigid body. Section 1.1.

Bound. The largest magnitude an input may take, written with the subscript max⁡\maxmax. The physical cause is called a limit of the actuator. Section 13.3.

Cable-suspended load. A point mass hanging from a quadrotor on a massless cable. A rigid body carried by several quadrotors is a payload. Section 2.7.

Cascade. A controller built as nested loops, each of which supplies the desired value for the next one in: position, then direction, then attitude. Section 6.5.

CBF-CLF-QP. A quadratic program solved at every instant, with a barrier condition as a hard constraint and the decrease of a Lyapunov function as a constraint with a slack. (8.16).

Central path. The curve of minimizers of a problem with a logarithmic barrier, as the barrier parameter decreases to zero. Section 13.7.

Compatible. A transport map is compatible with an error function when the error function changes only through the velocity error. Definition 3.5.

Configuration manifold. The set of all configurations of a system, with its curved structure. For a robot with several parts it is a product manifold. Section 2.7.

Constraint subspace. The subspace ker⁡C\ker CkerC in which the linear state of a system on S2S^2S2 lives. Section 7.3.

Coordinate-free. Written with the objects themselves (RRR, qqq) and not with a chart such as Euler angles. A geometric controller or model is a coordinate-free one. Section 1.6.

Cross term. The term c eR⋅JeΩc\,e_R\cdot J e_\OmegaceR​⋅JeΩ​ added to a Lyapunov function so that its rate is negative in the error vector as well as in the velocity error. (6.6).

Cut locus. The configurations from which the shortest way back to the target is not unique. Section 7.7.

Cutoff. The bandwidth kfk_fkf​ of the low-pass filter in L1 adaptive control. It sets what reaches the motors and how much delay the loop tolerates. (8.10).

Direct transcription. Trajectory optimization that discretizes first and then optimizes over states and inputs together, with the dynamics as constraints. Section 13.6.

Direction. A unit vector q∈S2q \in S^2q∈S2: a cable, a rod, a thrust axis. Section 1.5.

Double cover. The two-to-one map from unit quaternions to rotations. Section 1.3.

Equations of motion. The differential equations that give the accelerations of a system. Written out in words, never abbreviated. Chapter 4.

Error function. A nonnegative function Ψ\PsiΨ of the actual and desired configurations that vanishes only when they agree. The text also calls it a height. Definition 1.4, Definition 3.4.

Error state. The pair (χ,δΩ)(\chi, \delta\Omega)(χ,δΩ): the logarithm of the left-invariant error and the deviation of the velocity from the reference. It is the linear state in a form a controller can compute. Section 13.2.

Error-state MPC. Model predictive control whose model is the linearized dynamics of the error state. Its optimization problem is a quadratic program. Definition 13.5.

Error vector. The slope of the error function under a variation: eRe_ReR​, eqe_qeq​. Definition 3.4.

Exponential map. The map that wraps a tangent space onto the manifold. On a matrix group it is the matrix exponential. Section 2.3.

Exponential stability on a sublevel set. Convergence at a guaranteed exponential rate from every start inside a sublevel set of a Lyapunov function. The rate depends on the set and vanishes as the set grows to include half a turn. Theorem 6.1.

Feedforward. The part of a control law computed from the model and the reference and not from the error. One word, no hyphen. (6.2).

Force error. The difference eF=fR e3−Fde_F = f R\,e_3 - F_deF​=fRe3​−Fd​ between the force a thrust axis delivers and the force the position loop asked for. (6.15).

Forward invariant. A set is forward invariant when solutions that start in it stay in it. Section 8.6.

Gimbal lock. The loss of one direction of rotation in an Euler-angle description, at 90° of pitch for yaw, pitch and roll. Section 1.2.

Gradient, Hessian. The first- and second-order coefficients of a function along a step that stays on the manifold. Definition 13.1.

Half turn. A rotation by 180°. Half a turn away from the target is as far as an attitude can be. Section 1.1.

Hamilton’s principle. The motion makes the action stationary among paths with the same endpoints. Definition 4.1.

Hat map, vee map. The hat map turns a vector into the skew-symmetric matrix of its cross product. The vee map is its inverse. Definition 1.2.

Homogeneous space. A manifold on which a group acts so that any point can be moved to any other. The sphere is one, under rotations. Section 2.5.

Interior-point method. A method for inequality constraints that follows the central path to the solution from inside the constraints. Section 13.7.

KKT conditions. The Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions: the first-order conditions for a constrained minimum. (13.15).

L1 adaptive control. An adaptive architecture with a state predictor, a fast adaptation law and a low-pass filter between the estimate and the input. Written L1 in prose. The script letter is kept for the L1\mathcal{L}_1L1​ norm. Section 8.4.

Lagrange-d’Alembert principle. Hamilton’s principle with the virtual work of the applied forces added. Definition 4.2.

Left-invariant, right-invariant. Unchanged when both arguments are multiplied by the same group element on the left, or on the right. A left-invariant control law does not depend on the choice of world frame. Section 1.2, Definition 3.7.

Lie algebra. The tangent space of a Lie group at the identity. Definition 2.2.

Lie bracket. The operation on the Lie algebra that records the failure of the group to commute. On so(3)\so(3)so(3) it is the cross product. Section 2.4.

Lie group. A manifold that is also a group, with smooth multiplication and inversion. Definition 2.2.

Linear state. The state of a variation-based linearization: a variation and the variation of the velocity. Section 7.1.

Logarithm error. The attitude error vector log⁡(Rd⊤R)∨\log(R_d\T R)^\veelog(Rd⊤​R)∨, whose length is the error angle. Section 3.3.

Logarithmic barrier. The term −μln⁡x-\mu\ln x−μlnx that replaces a constraint x≥0x \ge 0x≥0 in a cost. It is not a barrier function. Section 13.7.

Lumped uncertainty. Everything a controller’s model gets wrong, collected in the one vector Δ\DeltaΔ that appears in the error dynamics. (8.3).

Manifold. A smooth curved set that looks like flat space near each of its points. Definition 2.1.

Matched uncertainty. An uncertainty that enters the dynamics at the same place as the input, so that a known one could be canceled. Section 8.2.

Model predictive control. Control by solving an optimization problem over a receding horizon at every sample. Abbreviated MPC. Definition 13.5.

Multicopter. A vehicle lifted by several rotors. A quadrotor is a multicopter with four.

Pitfall. A block that warns of a trap. It is always referred to by that name.

Plain controller. The tracking law of (6.2) with no adaptation and no safety filter. Section 8.1.

Prediction error. The error between the state predictor and the vehicle, measured with the error function, the error vector and the transport map. (8.5).

Product manifold. The set of lists of points, one from each factor. Section 2.7.

Quadratic program. The minimization of a quadratic cost under linear constraints. Abbreviated QP. (8.11), (13.11).

Receding horizon. Solving over the next NNN steps, applying the first input, and solving again at the next sample. Definition 13.5.

Reference. A desired motion that changes with time, marked by the subscript ddd. Section 3.4.

Relative degree. The number of times a function of the state must be differentiated before the input appears. Section 6.6, Section 8.7.

Restoring torque. The proportional part of an attitude controller, −kp e-k_p\,e−kp​e for an error vector eee. the pitfall.

Retraction. A rule that moves a point of a manifold along a tangent vector, lands on the manifold, and agrees with the exponential map to first order. Definition 13.2.

Safe set. The set of states a system must never leave, written {H≥0}\{H \ge 0\}{H≥0}. Section 8.6.

Safety filter. A program that changes a proposed input by the smallest amount that satisfies a barrier condition. (8.11).

Sampling period. The time between two updates of a sampled controller. It is the time step hhh. Section 13.3.

Slack variable. A variable www that turns an inequality into an equality, or lets a constraint be violated at a cost. A slack cable is a cable without tension. (8.16), (13.15).

State predictor. A copy of the believed dynamics, driven by the estimate of the uncertainty and run alongside the vehicle. The L1 literature calls it the reference model. Section 8.4.

Sublevel set. The set of states where a function is below a given value. A sublevel set of a Lyapunov function that never increases is a set no solution can leave. Section 6.2.

Tangent space, tangent plane. The vector space of velocities allowed at a point of a manifold. On the sphere it is a plane and is called the tangent plane. Definition 2.1.

Target. A desired configuration that does not move, marked by the subscript ddd. Section 1.5.

Trace error. The attitude error function 12tr⁡(I−Rd⊤R)\tfrac12\tr(I - R_d\T R)21​tr(I−Rd⊤​R) and its error vector eRe_ReR​. Section 3.3.

Transport map. A rule that carries a velocity at the desired configuration to a velocity at the actual one, so that the two can be subtracted. Definition 3.5.

Twist. An element of se(3)\se(3)se(3): a linear and an angular velocity, ζ=(V,Ω)\zeta = (V, \Omega)ζ=(V,Ω). Section 2.6.

Unwinding. A rotation the long way round, caused by a controller that treats q\quatq and −q-\quat−q as different attitudes. Section 1.3.

Variation. The first-order gap between a curve and its neighbors in a family of curves that stay on the manifold. The text also calls it a nudge. Definition 3.1.

Velocity error. The actual velocity minus the transported desired velocity: eΩe_\OmegaeΩ​, eωe_\omegaeω​. Definition 3.5.

Virtual work. The work the applied forces would do on a variation. Definition 4.2.

Warm start. Starting a solver from the solution of the previous problem. Section 13.4.

World frame. The frame that does not move, with e3e_3e3​ up. Section 1.1.

World-frame angular velocity. The angular velocity expressed in the world frame, ω\omegaω. Section 2.2.1.
