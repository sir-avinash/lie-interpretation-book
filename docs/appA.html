A. Notation and glossary
Appendix A

Notation and glossary

By relieving the brain of all unnecessary work, a good notation sets it free to concentrate on more advanced problems …

Alfred North Whitehead, An Introduction to Mathematics (1911)

This appendix is the reference for the whole book. Each symbol means one thing, each term has one name, and every chapter, figure, notebook and skill follows the tables below. Read Section A.1 once. Use Section A.2 to look up a symbol and Section A.3 to look up a word. The last column of each table points to the place where the entry is introduced.

A few letters are local and are not listed: x,y,zx, y, zx,y,z for arbitrary vectors in an identity, AAA for an arbitrary matrix, i,j,ki, j, ki,j,k for indices, nnn and NNN for counts. So are Π\PiΠ for a projection, β\betaβ and σ\sigmaσ in two exercises, and cic_ici​ for the entries of a matrix. They are named where they are used and mean nothing beyond that sentence.

A.1 Conventions

Frames. The world frame does not move, with e3e_3e3​ pointing up. The body frame is attached to a rigid body. A rotation matrix RRR maps body-frame components to world-frame components.

Gravity. Gravity acts along −e3-e_3−e3​. A mass mmm at position xxx has potential energy mg e3⋅xm g\,e_3\cdot xmge3​⋅x and weight −mg e3-m g\,e_3−mge3​.

Body and world velocities. The angular velocity of a rigid body is Ω\OmegaΩ, expressed in the body frame, with R˙=R Ω∧\dot R = R\,\Omega^\wedgeR˙=RΩ∧. Its world-frame components are ω=R Ω\omega = R\,\Omegaω=RΩ. The angular velocity of a direction q∈S2q \in S^2q∈S2 is ω\omegaω, in the world frame, with q˙=ω×q\dot q = \omega\times qq˙​=ω×q and ω⋅q=0\omega\cdot q = 0ω⋅q=0. Positions xxx and velocities v=x˙v = \dot xv=x˙ are in the world frame.

Hat and vee. The hat map is written after its argument: x∧y=x×yx^\wedge y = x\times yx∧y=x×y and (x∧)∨=x(x^\wedge)^\vee = x(x∧)∨=x. On a twist, ζ∧\zeta^\wedgeζ∧ is the 4×44\times44×4 matrix of (2.10).

Twists. A twist is ordered linear part first, ζ=(V,Ω)\zeta = (V, \Omega)ζ=(V,Ω), both parts in the body frame.

Cable direction. For a cable-suspended load, qqq points from the quadrotor to the load, so xQ=xL−l qx_Q = x_L - l\,qxQ​=xL​−lq. The cable tension TTT is positive when the cable pulls.

Desired quantities. The subscript ddd marks the configuration or motion that a controller aims for: RdR_dRd​, qdq_dqd​, xdx_dxd​, Ωd\Omega_dΩd​, ωd\omega_dωd​. A fixed one is called the target. A moving one is called the reference. No other mark (“ref”, “des”, a star) is used.

Order of arguments. The actual configuration comes first and the desired one second, in formulas and in code: ΨR\Psi_RΨR​ is built from Rd⊤RR_d\T RRd⊤​R, and a function is called as e_R(R, Rd).

Variations. A variation is written with δ\deltaδ. On SO(3)\SO(3)SO(3) it is δR=R η∧\delta R = R\,\eta^\wedgeδR=Rη∧ with η\etaη in the body frame and unconstrained. On S2S^2S2 it is δq=ξ×q\delta q = \xi\times qδq=ξ×q with ξ⋅q=0\xi\cdot q = 0ξ⋅q=0. The small parameter of a family of curves is ϵ\epsilonϵ.

Velocity variation on the sphere. With ω⋅q=0\omega\cdot q = 0ω⋅q=0 and ξ⋅q=0\xi\cdot q = 0ξ⋅q=0 kept along the whole curve, δω=ξ˙−2 ω×ξ\delta\omega = \dot\xi - 2\,\omega\times\xiδω=ξ˙​−2ω×ξ. This vector is not tangent: its tangent part is ξ˙−ω×ξ\dot\xi - \omega\times\xiξ˙​−ω×ξ and its part along qqq is −ω×ξ-\omega\times\xi−ω×ξ. See the pitfall.

Equations on the sphere. An equation of motion for q∈S2q \in S^2q∈S2 holds only after projection by I−qq⊤I - q q\TI−qq⊤, together with q⋅ω˙=0q\cdot\dot\omega = 0q⋅ω˙=0.

Signs of derived equations. Chapters write equations of motion with the inertia on the left, as in JΩ˙+Ω×JΩ=τJ\dot\Omega + \Omega\times J\Omega = \tauJΩ˙+Ω×JΩ=τ. The symbolic library of Chapter 5 returns the coefficient of each variation, Eξ=0E_\xi = 0Eξ​=0, with the inertia term negative, and its standard form is M v˙+b+G u=0M\,\dot v + b + G\,u = 0Mv˙+b+Gu=0.

Transposes and products. The transpose is R⊤R\TR⊤. The dot product of two vectors is a⋅ba\cdot ba⋅b, never a⊤ba\T ba⊤b. A transpose appears only in matrix expressions such as qq⊤q q\Tqq⊤ and s⊤Q ss\T Q\,ss⊤Qs. A norm is ∥x∥\|x\|∥x∥. The identity is III, with a subscript only when its size is not three.

Angles and units. Angles in formulas are in radians. Degrees are used in prose and on plot axes, written 90° in text and 90∘90^\circ90∘ in math. Units are SI and upright: rad/s, m/s², kg·m², N·m, N·s, s⁻¹. A percentage is written 5%.

Spelling. American spelling: linearization, modeling, stabilize, center, behavior, maneuver, neighbor.

Names in code. Notebooks, skills and scripts use hat, vee, expm_so3, log_so3, Psi_R, Psi_q, e_R, e_Omega, e_q, e_omega, and write desired quantities as Rd, qd, Omegad, omegad. Outside the book the same notation is written in plain LaTeX, for example R_d^\top R.

Colors in figures. Ink is structure. Indigo is the main object, the reference or the method that works. Terracotta is the contrasting object, the error or the method that fails. Teal is a third role. The trace error is always indigo and the logarithm error always teal.

A.3 Glossary

Action. The integral of the Lagrangian along a path. Section 4.1.

Adjoint. Conjugation by a group element, acting on the Lie algebra. It changes the frame of a velocity. Section 2.2.1.

Almost-global stability. Convergence from every initial condition except a set of measure zero. It is the best a continuous controller can do on a compact configuration space. Section 1.4.

Attitude. The rotation of a rigid body relative to the world frame, a point of SO(3)\SO(3)SO(3). Section 1.1.

Body angular velocity. The angular velocity Ω\OmegaΩ of a rigid body, expressed in the body frame. It is what a gyroscope reads. Section 2.2.1.

Body frame. The frame attached to a rigid body. Section 1.1.

Cable-suspended load. A point mass hanging from a quadrotor on a massless cable. A rigid body carried by several quadrotors is a payload. Section 2.7.

Compatible. A transport map is compatible with an error function when the error function changes only through the velocity error. Definition 3.5.

Configuration manifold. The set of all configurations of a system, with its curved structure. For a robot with several parts it is a product manifold. Section 2.7.

Constraint subspace. The subspace ker⁡C\ker CkerC in which the linear state of a system on S2S^2S2 lives. Section 7.3.

Coordinate-free. Written with the objects themselves (RRR, qqq) and not with a chart such as Euler angles. A geometric controller or model is a coordinate-free one. Section 1.6.

Cut locus. The configurations from which the shortest way back to the target is not unique. Section 7.7.

Direction. A unit vector q∈S2q \in S^2q∈S2: a cable, a rod, a thrust axis. Section 1.5.

Double cover. The two-to-one map from unit quaternions to rotations. Section 1.3.

Equations of motion. The differential equations that give the accelerations of a system. Written out in words, never abbreviated. Chapter 4.

Error function. A nonnegative function Ψ\PsiΨ of the actual and desired configurations that vanishes only when they agree. The text also calls it a height. Definition 1.4, Definition 3.4.

Error vector. The slope of the error function under a variation: eRe_ReR​, eqe_qeq​. Definition 3.4.

Exponential map. The map that wraps a tangent space onto the manifold. On a matrix group it is the matrix exponential. Section 2.3.

Gimbal lock. The loss of one direction of rotation in an Euler-angle description, at 90° of pitch for yaw, pitch and roll. Section 1.2.

Half turn. A rotation by 180°. Half a turn away from the target is as far as an attitude can be. Section 1.1.

Hamilton’s principle. The motion makes the action stationary among paths with the same endpoints. Definition 4.1.

Hat map, vee map. The hat map turns a vector into the skew-symmetric matrix of its cross product. The vee map is its inverse. Definition 1.2.

Homogeneous space. A manifold on which a group acts so that any point can be moved to any other. The sphere is one, under rotations. Section 2.5.

Lagrange-d’Alembert principle. Hamilton’s principle with the virtual work of the applied forces added. Definition 4.2.

Left-invariant, right-invariant. Unchanged when both arguments are multiplied by the same group element on the left, or on the right. A left-invariant control law does not depend on the choice of world frame. Section 1.2, Definition 3.7.

Lie algebra. The tangent space of a Lie group at the identity. Definition 2.2.

Lie bracket. The operation on the Lie algebra that records the failure of the group to commute. On so(3)\so(3)so(3) it is the cross product. Section 2.4.

Lie group. A manifold that is also a group, with smooth multiplication and inversion. Definition 2.2.

Linear state. The state of a variation-based linearization: a variation and the variation of the velocity. Section 7.1.

Logarithm error. The attitude error vector log⁡(Rd⊤R)∨\log(R_d\T R)^\veelog(Rd⊤​R)∨, whose length is the error angle. Section 3.3.

Manifold. A smooth curved set that looks like flat space near each of its points. Definition 2.1.

Multicopter. A vehicle lifted by several rotors. A quadrotor is a multicopter with four.

Pitfall. A block that warns of a trap. It is always referred to by that name.

Product manifold. The set of lists of points, one from each factor. Section 2.7.

Reference. A desired motion that changes with time, marked by the subscript ddd. Section 3.4.

Restoring torque. The proportional part of an attitude controller, −kp e-k_p\,e−kp​e for an error vector eee. the pitfall.

Tangent space, tangent plane. The vector space of velocities allowed at a point of a manifold. On the sphere it is a plane and is called the tangent plane. Definition 2.1.

Target. A desired configuration that does not move, marked by the subscript ddd. Section 1.5.

Trace error. The attitude error function 12tr⁡(I−Rd⊤R)\tfrac12\tr(I - R_d\T R)21​tr(I−Rd⊤​R) and its error vector eRe_ReR​. Section 3.3.

Transport map. A rule that carries a velocity at the desired configuration to a velocity at the actual one, so that the two can be subtracted. Definition 3.5.

Twist. An element of se(3)\se(3)se(3): a linear and an angular velocity, ζ=(V,Ω)\zeta = (V, \Omega)ζ=(V,Ω). Section 2.6.

Unwinding. A rotation the long way round, caused by a controller that treats q\quatq and −q-\quat−q as different attitudes. Section 1.3.

Variation. The first-order gap between a curve and its neighbors in a family of curves that stay on the manifold. The text also calls it a nudge. Definition 3.1.

Velocity error. The actual velocity minus the transported desired velocity: eΩe_\OmegaeΩ​, eωe_\omegaeω​. Definition 3.5.

Virtual work. The work the applied forces would do on a variation. Definition 4.2.

World frame. The frame that does not move, with e3e_3e3​ up. Section 1.1.

World-frame angular velocity. The angular velocity expressed in the world frame, ω\omegaω. Section 2.2.1.
